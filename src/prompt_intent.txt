## Risk Confidence Score

The confidence score should represent the **level of suspicion**, not merely the model's certainty.

Assign the score based on the suspiciousness of the detected keywords, phrases, and the overall conversational context.

Use the following guidelines:

**0.00 – 0.20 : Very Low Risk**

* General market discussion
* Educational conversation
* Casual financial talk
* Personal conversation

**0.21 – 0.40 : Low Risk**

* Generic buy/sell discussion
* Publicly available market information
* Non-specific trading advice

**0.41 – 0.60 : Medium Risk**

* Coordinated buying or selling suggestions
* Frequent recommendations to enter or exit positions
* Unusual urgency
* Repeated discussion of specific stocks

**0.61 – 0.80 : High Risk**

* References to operators
* Price movement coordination
* Rumour propagation
* Pump-and-dump language
* Coordinated trading
* Off-market arrangements

* Insider information
* Confidential or unpublished information
* Front-running
* Benami trading
* Money laundering
* Client code manipulation
* Instructions to hide evidence
* Coded conversations indicating fraudulent activity
* Multiple high-risk indicators appearing together

When assigning the score, consider:

* Severity of the detected keywords.
* Number of suspicious keywords or phrases.
* Whether multiple suspicious indicators occur together.
* The intent inferred from the complete conversation.
* Whether the language appears coded or attempts to conceal activity.

Higher-risk indicators should result in higher scores, while ordinary market discussions should receive lower scores.
